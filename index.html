Why this matters now

Your liability insurance may no longer cover AI mistakes.

Since January 2026, insurers can add standard exclusions to general liability policies for losses that arise from generative AI, which may apply even to AI used through a vendor. By July 31, 2026, insurers had filed 4,078 adoptions of these exclusions, 3,955 of them in lines small businesses commonly buy.1, 2 Ask your broker whether your policy has one.

What this is not

  1. Not a certification, a SOC 2 audit, or legal, financial or compliance advice.
  2. A check of a sample: errors outside it can exist.
  3. It checks your AI against your sources; it cannot prove a source is right.
  4. Money figures are estimates from your counts, not a promise of savings.

Protect yourself today

  1. For any number, date, name or quote you will act on, ask the AI for its source and open it yourself.
  2. Check the one or two claims your decision rests on against the original, not against another AI.
  3. Watch for dropped conditions: "effective" is not "effective if your own controls work."
  4. Ask what was left out, not only whether what was said is right.
  5. Log the AI errors you catch and what each cost. It becomes your own error rate.
